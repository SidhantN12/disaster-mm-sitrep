Three fusion mechanisms, trained identically on 20,000 tiles under a controlled protocol, compared on the same held-out tiles. Cross-attention is the only one whose writing responds to the field reports — correctly, on 16 of 16 tiles. Every output on this page came from the trained models.

03

Where the modalities meet

Everything before the highlighted block is shared and frozen across all three runs, so the fusion mechanism is the only variable in the comparison.

feature-level

Early fusion

image→ViT→concat + MLP→decoder
reports→text enc.↗

Both vectors are glued together into one code at the input. Nothing downstream can tell the modalities apart, and no mechanism can weigh one report against another.

decision-level

Late fusion

image→own encoder + decoder↘
gate→output
reports→own encoder + decoder↗

Two separate networks that never exchange a single activation. One learned scalar decides how much of each to believe — and in training it never moved off 50/50.

cross-attention

Hybrid fusion

patch tokens↘
co-attention→decoder
report tokens↗

Report words query image regions and image regions query report words, at every layer. Each generated word then picks its own evidence — which is what section 01 is drawing.

04

What the comparison found

A pretrained ViT-B/16 and BERT-base encoder pair (223M parameters) trained once as a multimodal autoencoder and then frozen, so the fusion mechanism is the only variable across the three runs. 20,000 training tiles, 20 stage-2 epochs per variant, evaluated on 2,000 held-out tiles. Inference costs 155 ms per tile.

Report relevance

weighted F1 · vertical rule is the always-say-yes baseline (0.530)

All three clear the baseline comfortably, and all three land on exactly 0.9671. On the one task that plainly needs the text, the fusion mechanism makes no difference at all.

The same task, every report deleted

weighted F1 after ablating the text modality

All three fall to 0.1690 — exactly the always-say-no baseline. So all three genuinely use the reports for classification. That is not where they differ.

Bridge at 0.31 and level at 0.94 are both just the modal answer. Counts are near zero everywhere. The only row where a mechanism separates is the last.

Cross-attention is the only mechanism whose writing is conditioned on the reports. Of the 16 tiles shown above, 10 carry field reports and 6 carry none. Hybrid lists needs on exactly those 10 and writes “none reported” on exactly those 6 — 16 out of 16. Early and late fusion write “none reported” on all sixteen, including a tile with seven reports attached to it. Late fusion's own learned gate says why: its damage and bag gates sat at 0.499/0.501, never moving from initialisation, but its SITREP gate converged to 0.990 / 0.010 — it routed generation almost entirely to one branch and stopped listening to the other.
A metric finding worth carrying forward. Hybrid scores BLEU 60.09 with the reports and 66.07 with them deleted — conditioning on the evidence makes the output more informative and the score worse, because “none reported” is the modal ending and matches more references. Ranking these three by BLEU selects the two that ignore the text. The ablation protocol used here is what separates them correctly.
The headline number on damage is the label's, not the model's. All three mechanisms score 0.9323 weighted F1 — and so does always answering “destroyed”, to four decimal places, because 95% of tiles carry that label once a tile inherits the worst damage among its structures. Measuring against the constant-predictor baseline is what makes this visible at all; a paper reporting 0.93 without it would look like a strong result. The fix is a per-structure target rather than a tile-level maximum.
